# Reputation Systems in 2D Gaming

A **Reputation System** is a gameplay mechanic that tracks a player's choices and actions to determine how the game world—and its inhabitants—react to them. It serves as an invisible "social scoreboard" that bridges the gap between mechanical gameplay and narrative consequence.

---

## 1. Core Mechanics
Unlike linear games where NPCs treat you the same regardless of your actions, a reputation system introduces **consequential gameplay** through several methods:

* **The Karma Meter:** A sliding scale between two extremes (e.g., "Good" vs. "Evil").
* **Faction Standing:** Tracking loyalty to specific groups. Gaining favor with one often results in a penalty with their rivals.
* **Action Triggers:** Reputation shifts based on:
    * Completing or failing specific quests.
    * Combat choices (sparing vs. executing enemies).
    * Environmental interactions (stealing, trespassing, or donating).

---

## 2. Gameplay Impact
A well-designed system changes the 2D world in tangible, mechanical ways:

| Feature | Low Reputation / "Evil" | High Reputation / "Good" |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Merchant Prices** | Prices skyrocket; some refuse to trade. | Significant discounts and "special stock" unlocked. |
| **NPC Behavior** | Characters may flee, hide, or call guards. | Characters offer tips, gifts, and warm greetings. |
| **World Access** | Banned from certain cities or zones. | Keys to restricted areas or VIP lounges granted. |
| **Followers** | Recruit mercenaries and outlaws. | Recruit heroes and legendary companions. |

---

## 3. Iconic 2D Examples
* **Undertale:** Tracks every monster killed or spared, leading to the "Pacifist," "Neutral," or "Genocide" routes.
* **Stardew Valley:** A social-focused system where "Heart Levels" unlock unique story beats, recipes, and marriage options.
* **RimWorld:** Manages complex relationships with neighboring factions based on trade, prisoner treatment, and diplomacy.
* **Fallout 1 & 2:** Classic isometric RPGs where your "Reputation" and "Karma" determine which towns welcome you and which send bounty hunters after you.

---

## 4. The Value of the System
* **Replayability:** Encourages players to start new files to see "the other side" of the story.
* **Immersion:** Ensures the world feels reactive. If a player robs a shopkeeper and is treated like a hero the next day, the "internal logic" of the game breaks.
* **Visual Feedback:** In 2D games, reputation is often signaled by changes in the player's sprite (e.g., darker colors, a change in expression) or shifting UI elements.

> **Pro-Tip:** Balancing reputation is key. If the "Evil" path offers no mechanical benefits (like extra gold or power), players will rarely choose it unless they are role-playing specifically for the story.
